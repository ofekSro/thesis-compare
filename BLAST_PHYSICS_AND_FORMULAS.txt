================================================================================
  URBAN BLAST CONVERGENCE RADIUS — PHYSICAL CONCLUSIONS AND FINAL FORMULAS
  compare_v6  |  96 configurations  |  Master's thesis (Ofek Rotem)
================================================================================

Data source: convergence_table.csv — 96 configurations
  = 2 detonation types × 18 geometries × charge weights 50/250/500/1000/1500 kg
RadiusP / RadiusI = distance from detonation where the urban blast field
converges to the free-field blast (peak overpressure / impulse).

Notation used throughout:
  W    = charge weight [kg TNT]
  b    = building size [m],  s = street width [m],  H = building height [m]
  rho  = plan area density = b²/(b+s)²
  det1 = street detonation (between buildings)
  det2 = intersection detonation
  Z    = R / W^(1/3)  — Hopkinson-Cranz scaled convergence radius
  Pi   = H / (s·rho) — confinement index
  W^(1/3) values: 50 kg → 3.68 m/kg^(1/3),  500 kg → 7.94,  1500 kg → 11.45


================================================================================
  PART I — BUCKINGHAM PI FRAMEWORK
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. DERIVATION OF PI GROUPS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Physical variables governing the problem:

    R   [m]         convergence radius (dependent variable)
    E   [J]         charge energy, proportional to W [kg TNT]
    p0  [Pa]        ambient pressure
    b   [m]         building size
    s   [m]         street width
    H   [m]         building height

6 variables, 3 independent dimensions (M, L, T)  →  6 − 3 = 4 dimensionless
groups (3 independent + 1 dependent).

Choosing E and p0 as repeating variables gives the natural blast length scale

    L* = (E/p0)^(1/3)  ∝  W^(1/3)        (Hopkinson–Cranz scaling law)

Every length in the problem is measured against W^(1/3). The four groups are:

    Π₀ = R / W^(1/3)  = Z              (scaled convergence radius — target)
    Π₁ = rho = b²/(b+s)²               (plan area density)
    Π₂ = s / W^(1/3)                   (scaled street width — regime variable)
    Π₃ = H / s                         (street-canyon aspect ratio)

Buckingham's theorem guarantees:  Z = f( rho, s/W^(1/3), H/s ; det )

The theorem fixes WHAT the formula can depend on; the data tells us the SHAPE.

Note on dimensionality: strictly, (E/p0)^(1/3) carries units m/kg^(1/3). Since
all simulations share one atmosphere and one explosive type, the constant is
absorbed into the fitted coefficients. To generalize to other ambient pressures,
replace W^(1/3) with (E/p0)^(1/3) throughout.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1b. DATA EXTRACTION — HOW THE CONVERGENCE RADII WERE MEASURED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The target R (= RadiusP / RadiusI per config) is extracted from the simulation
grids in find_convergence_radius.py. It is the distance beyond which the urban
field is indistinguishable from the free-field blast.

INPUT FIELD — the urban/free-field RATIO (not the absolute load):
  For every cell of the three merged-resolution grids, the ratio is formed in
  process_grids.py:
      ratioP = peakP_urban / peakP_freefield     (peak overpressure)
      ratioI = impulse_urban / impulse_freefield  (impulse)
  Convergence means ratio → 1. This is a RELATIVE criterion — it asks where the
  city stops perturbing the wave, independent of the absolute load level.

NEAR-FIELD EXCLUSION:
  Cells within exclude_r of the detonation are dropped (they sit under/inside
  buildings where the ratio is meaningless):
      det=1 (street):       exclude_r = sqrt((b/2)² + (s/2)²)  (corner of the
                            half-block straddling the charge)
      det=2 (intersection): exclude_r = s/2                    (half street width)

PER-ANGLE SCAN (91 one-degree bins, 0–90°, same binning as MaxR):
  Within each angular slice the cells are sorted FAR → NEAR and scanned inward.
  A running streak counts consecutive cells with |ratio − 1| > 5% (TOLERANCE).
  Non-convergence is declared only after K = 3 consecutive violations (so a
  single noisy cell cannot trigger it); the slice's convergence radius is the
  distance of the last converged cell just before that streak began. This yields
  one radius r_i per angle (NaN if the slice never converges).

AGGREGATION — Equivalent Area Radius (one scalar per config):
  The 91 per-angle radii are combined into the radius of a quarter-circle that
  encloses the SAME AREA as the polygon they sweep:
      A   = Σ ½ · r_i² · Δθ        (area of the swept polygon)
      Req = sqrt(4A / π)           (radius of a quarter-circle of equal area)
  Req is the reported RadiusP / RadiusI.

  What Req IS, precisely: with a fixed Δθ = 1° over ~90 bins, the algebra gives
  Req² = ⟨r_i²⟩, so Req is the ROOT-MEAN-SQUARE of the per-angle radii — NOT an
  outlier-robust statistic. Being RMS it weights long radii MORE heavily than
  the arithmetic mean (RMS ≥ mean always). This is DELIBERATE, not an artifact:
  the target is the affected-AREA footprint, area integrates r², so r²-weighting
  is the definition of "equal area." A median or percentile would answer a
  different question (typical angular reach) and would UNDER-state the footprint
  of an anisotropic, channeled field by discarding the long street-axis spikes.

  Magnitude (measured over all 96 configs, Req vs simpler summaries):
    Req / arithmetic-mean  : 1.03–1.08   (pure RMS-vs-mean emphasis — small)
    Req / median, PRESSURE : s=5 (channeling) 1.23 avg, up to 3.2;
                             s=20 (open) 1.09;  mid widths 1.07
    Req / median, IMPULSE  : 1.03–1.06 across all street widths
  The large Req/median for some narrow-street PRESSURE configs is not RMS
  "blowing up" (Req/mean is only ~1.08 even there) — it is a SKEWED angular
  profile: most directions are blocked and converge at short range, a few
  street-axis directions channel far, so the MEDIAN sits low. The area-
  equivalent radius keeps the channeled reach in proportion to the area it adds;
  the median would discard it entirely. The channeling signal the formula fits
  is therefore a real anisotropy, not an extraction artifact — but its
  coefficient MAGNITUDE does depend on this area-based summary (a median-based
  target would yield a smaller, still-positive canyon term). Impulse, whose
  field is far more isotropic, is insensitive to the choice either way.

AGGREGATION METHOD SWITCH — three selectable definitions of the radius:
  How the 91 per-angle radii r_i are collapsed into the single reported value is
  a one-line switch in find_convergence_radius.py (METHOD = 'eq_area'). All three
  are computed and compared side-by-side by convergence_radius_methods.py, which
  writes pressure_radius_methods.csv / impulse_radius_methods.csv and per-config
  figures into Figures_Radius_Methods/.

    'max'      R = max_i r_i
               The single farthest converged angle. Most conservative, but the
               NOISIEST — one channeled street or outlier slice fixes the whole
               radius.

    'p95'      R = 95th percentile of {r_i}
               A robust near-max: keeps near-worst-case directional reach while
               discarding the top ~5% of angular outliers.

    'eq_area'  R = sqrt(4A/π),  A = Σ ½ r_i² Δθ        ← CHOSEN (default)
               The equal-area / RMS radius defined above (the affected-AREA
               footprint). Smooth angular integral over all 91 bins.

  Ordering across configs: max ≥ p95 ≥ eq_area. The gap WIDENS for anisotropic
  (narrow-street, channeled) configs and shrinks to ≈1 for isotropic ones — it
  is a direct measure of how directional each field is.

  WHY eq_area IS USED: the three are monotonic summaries of the SAME angular
  profile, so the PHYSICAL CONCLUSIONS are invariant to the choice — the
  Buckingham-Pi formula structure, the coefficient SIGNS, and the regime
  boundary s = W^(1/3) (hard-coded, not fitted) are unchanged; only coefficient
  MAGNITUDES rescale (max/p95 amplify the channeling term by emphasising the
  street axis). The deciding factor is fit quality: switching to 'p95' keeps the
  test MAPE good but LOWERS R². p95 is a tail-driven statistic that injects
  angular-tail noise a smooth geometry formula cannot reproduce — this raises the
  unmodellable variance (penalised by R² = 1 − SS_res/SS_tot, a squared-ABSOLUTE-
  error metric) while predictions stay accurate in PERCENTAGE terms (MAPE).
  'eq_area' is the smooth angular integral, giving the best variance-explained
  fit, and is the reported RadiusP / RadiusI.

CONTRAST WITH THE MaxR EXTRACTION (used for Z_urban, see §10b):
  Both use the same 91 angular bins, but answer different questions:
    Convergence radius : RELATIVE ratio → 1 (±5%), far→near consecutive-
                         violation scan, aggregated by equivalent AREA.
                         One value per config.
    MaxR (Z_urban)     : ABSOLUTE exceedance of a free-field LEVEL (≥ ff_val),
                         outer boundary per angle, aggregated by 95th
                         PERCENTILE over angles. One value per (config, Z_free).

  Note: §10b dropped a ±5% tolerance BAND for MaxR, so it is fair to ask why a
  ±5% tolerance survives here. The answer is that the two use it differently and
  the failure mode does NOT transfer:
    • The rejected MaxR rule was a band COLLECTOR — match |val − ff_val| ≤
      5%·ff_val, then take the OUTERMOST matching cell. Near convergence the
      field is radially flat, so a thick ring falls inside the value-band and
      the outermost cell overshoots: flatness → inflation.
    • The convergence radius is a band EDGE-DETECTOR — a far→near scan for the
      sustained INNER edge of the converged region (K=3 consecutive
      violations). The flat converged region that broke MaxR is harmless here:
      the scan simply walks through it with the streak at zero. The radius is
      pinned by the SHARP inner transition (cells just inside violate by far
      more than 5%), not by the flat outer field.
    • Roles differ: for MaxR the 5% was an estimator BANDWIDTH on a
      tolerance-free target (biased → replaced). Here the 5% is the DEFINITION
      of "indistinguishable from free-field" — there is no tolerance-free
      convergence radius it approximates.

TOLERANCE ROBUSTNESS (verified):
  Re-extracting the convergence radii with TOLERANCE = 3% / 5% / 7% (more than
  doubling the band width) moves RadiusP by 0% and RadiusI by 0% in almost
  every config, with a worst case of −5% for one wide-street impulse config
  (config_12, W=1500), where impulse genuinely has a gradual tail near
  convergence. The result snaps to grid resolution because the inner transition
  is sharp — confirming the radius is set by the edge location, not the band
  width. (For comparison, the MaxR band method shifted results by 3–12% near
  convergence — the failure that motivated the exceedance switch in §10b.)


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. PHYSICAL MEANING OF EACH PI GROUP — AND HOW IT APPEARS IN THE DATA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
  Π₀ = Z = R / W^(1/3)   (HOPKINSON-CRANZ SCALED CONVERGENCE RADIUS)
══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Physical meaning:
  The convergence radius measured in units of the natural blast length scale.
  If urban geometry had NO effect on propagation, we would expect R ∝ W^(1/3)
  (the free-field Hopkinson law) and Z would be a geometry-only constant.
  Any Z that varies with W signals a geometry-charge interaction — i.e., the
  urban effect is not purely scale-invariant.

Why this group is the right target:
  Dividing out W^(1/3) collapses the enormous dynamic range of R (25 m at W=50
  to 170 m at W=1500) to a much tighter range of Z (roughly 5–15) that
  isolates the geometry signal from the charge-size effect.

What the data shows:
  • Z is NOT perfectly constant with W — it varies by up to ±30% across W
    values within the same geometry.
  • At small W (50 kg) Z clusters tightly around 6–8 regardless of geometry.
    The urban field is barely perturbed by the small charge.
  • At large W (≥500 kg) Z spreads widely (6–11) and correlates strongly
    with H/s and rho — the charge now engages the full canyon geometry.
  • Z is non-monotonic in W for geometries with high Pi (tall, narrow, dense):
    e.g., det1_b15_s5_h24: Z = 6.1 (W=50) → 9.2 (W=500) → 8.9 (W=1500).
    The confinement amplifies Z at W=500 but the effect saturates by W=1500.
  • For low-Pi configs (wide streets, low buildings): Z rises gently with W
    e.g., det1_b15_s20_h4: Z = 6.4 (W=50) → 7.3 (W=500) → 7.8 (W=1500).

Key conclusion: the Hopkinson scale W^(1/3) IS the correct normalization for
the problem — it removes 90% of the W-dependence and reveals the geometry signal.

══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
  Π₂ = s / W^(1/3)   (SCALED STREET WIDTH — THE REGIME VARIABLE)
══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Physical meaning:
  The ratio of street width to the natural blast length scale. Equivalently,
  Π₂ = 1/Π₂ = W^(1/3)/s tells us whether the charge is large or small relative
  to the street. This group controls the SIGN of every geometric effect:

  Π₂ > 1  (s > W^(1/3)): street is wide relative to the charge → the wave
    fits within the street without reaching the building walls; buildings are
    "far-field obstacles." Regime A: small-charge, absorption-dominated.

  Π₂ < 1  (s < W^(1/3)): charge is large relative to street → the initial
    blast envelope already envelops the buildings; walls become active waveguide
    surfaces. Regime B: large-charge, channeling-dominated.

  Π₂ = 1  (s = W^(1/3)): the regime boundary. Geometry has ZERO net effect
    on the scaled radius; Z collapses to the free-field baseline A.

Crossover charge for each street width (W_crit = s³):
  s = 5 m  → W_crit ≈ 125 kg  (between our W=50 and W=250 data points)
  s = 8 m  → W_crit ≈ 512 kg  (between W=250 and W=1000)
  s = 12 m → W_crit ≈ 1728 kg (barely above W=1500 — rarely crossed)
  s = 20 m → W_crit ≈ 8000 kg (NEVER crossed in our 96 configs)
  Important: for all our s=20 configurations, Π₂ > 1 at every W. We always
  observe "regime A" physics at wide streets, and the crossover at s=20 would
  require charges far beyond 1500 kg.

How Π₂ appears in the formula:
  • In the density term: rho·(s/W^(1/3) − a) → Π₂-dependent bracket flips sign.
  • In the canyon term: sqrt(rho)·(H/s)·(W^(1/3)/s − 1) → (1/Π₂ − 1) flips sign.
  • In the impulse formula: it is the exponent k·ln(W^(1/3)/s) = −k·ln(Π₂).

What the data shows — the TRUE signature of Π₂=1 is the SIGN FLIP of the
geometric sensitivities ∂R/∂(H/s) and ∂R/∂rho, NOT the location of any peak
in Z itself (see §3 for why Z's non-monotonicity is a separate phenomenon):
  ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │ Sign of height effect on RadiusP  (det1, b=15, W=500, Π₂ = s/7.94)     │
  │                                                                           │
  │ s=5  (Π₂=0.63 < 1):  h4 → h24: R = 56 → 73 m  (+31%)  CHANNELING ↑    │
  │ s=20 (Π₂=2.52 > 1):  h4 → h24: R = 58 → 51 m  (−12%)  BLOCKING  ↓    │
  └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
  Verified across the full weight sweep (the height-sensitivity plot is the
  primary evidence): for every s=5 family the slope ∂R/∂(H/s) crosses zero
  between W=50 (Π₂=1.36, blocking, slope < 0) and W=250 (Π₂=0.79, channeling,
  slope > 0) — i.e. exactly at the crossover charge W_crit = s³ = 125 kg, where
  Π₂≈1. For s=20, Π₂ stays > 1 at every weight (W_crit = 8000 kg), so the slope
  never crosses into channeling and the height effect remains negative
  throughout. The sign of the height effect is therefore determined purely by
  which side of Π₂=1 the configuration sits on. The density sensitivity
  ∂R/∂rho flips sign at the same boundary (clean for det1; noisier for det2 —
  see Π₁ section below).

══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
  Π₃ = H / s   (STREET-CANYON ASPECT RATIO)
══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Physical meaning:
  The shape of the street canyon — how deep the "slot" is relative to its
  width. A high Π₃ means a deep, narrow canyon (like a slot gorge); a low Π₃
  means a shallow, open corridor.

  Π₃ is the primary waveguide descriptor: it determines the solid angle
  subtended by the building walls as seen from a point in the street. High Π₃
  → walls dominate the visible angle → strong multiple-reflection channeling.

  Note that Π₃ depends only on the RATIO H/s, not on H or s individually. A
  24 m building over a 36 m street (Π₃=0.67, "low") behaves very differently
  from the same building over a 5 m street (Π₃=4.8, "deep slot").

How Π₃ appears in the formula:
  In the canyon term as H/s, multiplied by the sign-switching bracket and by
  the wall-continuity factor sqrt(rho). The HEIGHT itself never appears alone
  — always as the ratio H/s.

What the data shows:
  The HEIGHT EFFECT FLIPS SIGN AT THE REGIME BOUNDARY:

  Case 1 — Narrow street, large charge (s=5, W=500, Π₂=0.63 < 1):
    Increasing H/s from 0.8 (h=4) to 4.8 (h=24) INCREASES RadiusP:
      det1_b15_s5: R = 56.1 (h4) → 69.8 (h12) → 73.3 m (h24)   +31%
      det1_b30_s5: R = 48.8 (h4) → 64.8 (h12) → 80.5 m (h24)   +65%
    Physical reason: high walls act as a continuous waveguide; the shock wave
    bounces between walls and is channeled far down the street.

  Case 2 — Wide street, large charge (s=20, W=500, Π₂=2.52 > 1):
    Same height variation DECREASES RadiusP:
      det1_b15_s20: R = 57.9 (h4) → 53.1 (h12) → 51.3 m (h24)   −12%
      det1_b30_s20: R = 55.8 (h4) → 53.3 (h12) → 53.6 m (h24)    −4%
    Physical reason: at wide streets the blast clears the buildings; taller
    buildings become isolated obstacles that absorb and scatter energy, reducing
    the range over which the urban field differs from free-field.

  Case 3 — Narrow street, small charge (s=5, W=50, Π₂=1.36 > 1):
    Height effect is WEAK (near the boundary):
      det1_b15_s5: R = 25.2 (h4) → 22.5 (h12) → 22.5 m (h24)    −11%
    Physical reason: the small charge sits inside the street without reaching
    the walls; the canyon barely affects propagation. We are in regime A.

  Bottom line: Π₃ (H/s) only matters in the large-charge regime (Π₂ < 1),
  and even there its sign depends on how narrow the street is relative to W^(1/3).

══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
  Π₁ = rho = b²/(b+s)²   (PLAN AREA DENSITY)
══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Physical meaning:
  The fraction of ground-level area covered by buildings. Equivalently,
  rho = (b/(b+s))², the square of the linear filling fraction.

  Physically, rho measures how much of the urban fabric is "solid" vs "void":
    rho → 1: nearly all ground is covered by buildings (very dense city)
    rho → 0: sparse buildings with wide open spaces

  In our dataset: rho ranges from 0.184 (b=15, s=20) to 0.735 (b=30, s=5).

  Why it is rho = b²/(b+s)² and not some other density measure:
  This is the 2-D plan area ratio. An alternative 1-D measure — used for
  the channeling term — is b/(b+s) = sqrt(rho), which captures how continuous
  the wall surface is along the street (relevant for waveguide leakage).

How rho appears in the formula:
  1. In the density-switch term: C2·rho·(s/W^(1/3) − a)
     Here rho weights the overall blocking/reflecting power of the building mass.
  2. In the canyon term as sqrt(rho) = b/(b+s):
     The 1-D wall continuity — how much of the canyon wall is actually solid.
     A building row with large gaps between buildings "leaks" the guided wave.

The sign of the density effect reverses at Π₂ = a (regime boundary):

  Regime A (s/W^(1/3) > a, i.e., street wider than a Hopkinson lengths):
    The bracket (s/W^(1/3) − a) is POSITIVE → C2·rho > 0 → higher rho INCREASES Z.
    Physical reason: buildings are in the "far field" of the charge; they act
    as reflectors that keep feeding the blast back into the street, sustaining
    the urban perturbation over a larger distance.

  Regime B (s/W^(1/3) < a, i.e., street narrower than a Hopkinson lengths):
    The bracket is NEGATIVE → higher rho DECREASES Z.
    Physical reason: buildings are inside the strong-blast zone; a denser city
    means more frontal surface area absorbing momentum, more energy taken out of
    the wave close to the charge → the field decays to free-field sooner.

What the data shows:
  ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │ Density sign flip  (det1, s=5, h=4, changing b from 15→30)              │
  │                                                                           │
  │  rho(b=15) = 0.5625,  rho(b=30) = 0.7347                                │
  │                                                                           │
  │  W=50  (Π₂=1.36 > 1):  R_b15=25.2, R_b30=29.7  →  +18%  rho↑ → R↑    │
  │  W=500 (Π₂=0.63 < 1):  R_b15=56.1, R_b30=48.8  →  −13%  rho↑ → R↓    │
  └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
  At W=50 (regime A): denser city means more reflections feeding the blast
  → larger convergence radius. At W=500 (regime B): denser city means more
  frontal blocking → smaller convergence radius. The sign flip is clean.

  For wide streets (s=20) the effect is always regime A (Π₂ > 1 for all W≤1500),
  so density always increases Z at s=20. However the absolute effect is smaller
  because the canyon-wall continuity factor sqrt(rho) is lower for the s=20 cases.

  KEY FINDING: the density effect nearly vanishes for large charges at s=5.
  At W=1500 (Π₂=0.44), the switch bracket is −0.56, but the effect on Z is
  only ≈ −0.5–1.0 units — the canyon term dominates instead. Density matters
  most when H/W^(1/3) is large (small charges, tall buildings).

══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
  COMBINED GROUP: Π_conf = H / (s·rho)   (CONFINEMENT INDEX)
══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Physical meaning:
  Π_conf = Π₃ · (1 + 1/Π₄)²  combines the canyon aspect ratio and the density
  into a single number that measures how "trapped" the blast wave is:

    High Π_conf: tall buildings (high H/s) AND/OR sparse sidewalls (low rho)
                 → wave is trapped vertically AND not bled by cross-street gaps.
    Low Π_conf:  low canyon OR high density (solid walls at low height)
                 → wave "sees" solid surfaces but they are short, less trapping.

  A key identity:  H/(s·rho) = (H/s) × (b+s)²/b²
  This shows that wide buildings relative to their spacing (large Π₄=b/s)
  reduce Π_conf — counter-intuitively, MORE solid walls (high rho) means LESS
  confinement because the numerator H/s is divided by rho.

  Physically: what matters for channeling is the OPEN SPACE above the buildings
  relative to the street. A canyon that is 24 m tall and 5 m wide (Π₃=4.8) but
  has solid walls (rho→1) channels strongly. But if those walls have many gaps
  (low rho), energy escapes laterally and channeling is weaker.

How it enters the formula:
  Π_conf is the natural variable for the IMPULSE formula:
    ln(Z_I) = ln(A) + k·ln(Π_conf)·ln(W^(1/3)/s)
  A full 6-coefficient log-linear fit of both ln(H/s) and ln(rho) as separate
  terms recovers coefficients c₄ ≈ −c₅, confirming algebraically that only
  their ratio H/(s·rho) carries information. The two groups collapse to one.

  For the PRESSURE formula, Π_conf does not enter algebraically as a single
  ratio — the additive switch formula keeps H/s and rho in separate terms with
  different roles (canyon waveguide vs bulk blocking). But the same physical
  compression happens: the data only has 2 degrees of geometric freedom per
  regime, and Π_conf is one of them.

What the data shows — the Π_conf collapse for impulse:
  Configs with equal Π_conf but different individual H, s, and rho should give
  approximately equal Z_I at the same W. Two geometries:
    b15_s20_h12 (Π₃=0.6, rho=0.184): Π_conf = 0.6/0.184 = 3.26
    b30_s5_h12  (Π₃=2.4, rho=0.735): Π_conf = 2.4/0.735 = 3.26   ← same!
  Despite very different individual parameters, both have Π_conf=3.26.
  Impulse radii at W=500:
    b15_s20_h12: RadiusI = 89.4 m → Z_I = 11.26
    b30_s5_h12:  RadiusI = 96.9 m → Z_I = 12.21
  The values are close but not identical — the venting term (Π₂) differs
  (s/W^(1/3) = 2.52 vs 0.63), which is the other Pi group that enters.


================================================================================
  PART II — PHYSICAL CONCLUSIONS
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. MONOTONICITY WITH CHARGE WEIGHT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

CONCLUSION: For identical geometry, RadiusP and RadiusI grow monotonically with
W — 0 violations in all 36 geometry groups.

This is the direct manifestation of Hopkinson-Cranz scaling: more energy →
larger blast footprint at every scale. Urban geometry modulates the RATE of
growth but cannot reverse it.

HOW GROWTH RATE CHANGES ACROSS THE WEIGHT RANGE:

  50 → 500 kg (factor 10 in W, factor 2.15 in W^(1/3)):
    R grows FASTER than W^(1/3) on average — factor ×1.23 in R (expected ×2.15
    from simple Hopkinson); at intersections ×1.39 the Hopkinson prediction.
    Reason: crossing the regime boundary (s = W^(1/3)) "switches on" the canyon
    channeling effect. As W grows from 50 to 500 the charge starts engaging the
    building walls, adding an urban amplification on top of the basic scaling.

  500 → 1500 kg (factor 3 in W, factor 1.44 in W^(1/3)):
    R grows nearly EXACTLY as W^(1/3) — factor ×1.00 normalized (saturation).
    Some tall-building, narrow-street configs slightly below (×0.95 in Z).
    Reason: by W=500 most channeling configurations are already in deep regime B;
    growing W further deepens the regime but doesn't open new mechanisms.

CONSEQUENCE — Z IS NON-MONOTONIC (a SEPARATE phenomenon from the Π₂=1 crossing):
  Z = R/W^(1/3) is itself not monotonic in W for high-Pi geometries: it rises,
  then falls back toward an asymptotic value. This happens in 17 of 36 groups,
  all with at least moderate Pi (H/s > 1, narrow streets).

  IMPORTANT — do NOT read the Z-peak as the regime boundary. The two are
  distinct events:
    • The regime crossing Π₂ = 1 (s = W^(1/3)) for s=5 is at W_crit = s³ = 125 kg,
      i.e. between W=50 and W=250. Its signature is the sensitivity sign-flip
      (§Π₂), which has already happened by W=250.
    • The rise-then-fall of Z is the canyon term first switching on (channeling
      adds super-Hopkinson growth) and then SATURATING at large W; the turnover
      sits deep inside regime B, NOT on the boundary.

  Example: det1_b15_s5_h24   (W_crit = 125 kg, so Π₂ < 1 for all W ≥ 250)
    Z(W=50)  = 22.5/3.68  = 6.1   (Π₂ = 1.36 > 1 — still regime A)
    Z(W=500) = 73.3/7.94  = 9.2   (Π₂ = 0.63 < 1 — DEEP in regime B, not the
                                    boundary; this peak is channeling saturation)
    Z(W=1500)= 101.8/11.45 = 8.9  (Π₂ = 0.44 — slight fall-back)
  The regime crossing itself (Π₂=1) lies between the first two points, near
  W=125 — invisible in Z, but visible as the height-sensitivity sign-flip.

  Example (low Pi): det1_b15_s20_h4
    Z(W=50)  = 23.5/3.68  = 6.4
    Z(W=500) = 57.9/7.94  = 7.3
    Z(W=1500)= 88.9/11.45 = 7.8  ← monotonically rising (s=20 never reaches
                                    Π₂=1 for W ≤ 1500 kg, so no channeling
                                    switch-on and no turnover)


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. INTERSECTION VS STREET DETONATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

CONCLUSION: At large charges (W ≥ 250 kg) intersection radii exceed street
radii by 8–24%. At W=50 the relationship depends on geometry.

Physical reason: at an intersection the blast feeds TWO sets of orthogonal
canyons (4 directions) instead of one straight canyon (2 directions). The two
canyon axes act independently as waveguides, so the total "area of influence"
is larger. This is encoded in:
  - The larger C0 for det=2 (10.1 vs 7.8) — 4-canyon baseline
  - The larger a=2 threshold (vs a=1) — both canyon axes must vent simultaneously
  - The k_intersection ≈ 2·k_street pattern for the log impulse formula
    (2 independent confinement channels)

At W=50 (regime A) the intersection VENTS more freely for wide-street configs:
  det1_b15_s20_h4_w50: R = 23.5 m
  det2_b15_s20_h4_w50: R = 18.5 m  (−21%)
The blast at an intersection has 4 escape directions, so for a small charge
in an open layout the urban effect is WEAKER at the intersection. The effect
reverses when the charge grows large enough to exploit the 4 canyons.

For narrow streets even at W=50, det=2 > det=1 because the 4 canyons already
confine the small charge:
  det1_b15_s5_h4_w50: R = 25.2 m
  det2_b15_s5_h4_w50: R = 29.4 m  (+16%)


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. IMPULSE CONVERGENCE RADIUS VS PRESSURE CONVERGENCE RADIUS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

CONCLUSION: RadiusI > RadiusP in nearly every configuration (impulse converges
farther from the detonation point than peak overpressure does).

Physical reason: peak overpressure decays rapidly with distance and converges to
free-field as soon as the strongest portion of the shock front reaches open
space. Impulse is a TIME INTEGRAL of the positive pressure phase — it accumulates
the contribution of all reflections, channeled flows, and late-arriving waves.
Even after the peak has equalized, the integral continues to differ between
urban and free-field environments until all reflections have propagated out.
The convergence of impulse therefore happens farther from the source.

Numerically: RadiusI / RadiusP ≈ 1.5–2.0 across all configurations. The ratio
is largest for narrow-street, tall-building configs at large W (deepest channeling
creates the longest-lived reflections).


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. SUPER-HOPKINSON SCALING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

CONCLUSION: Some geometries show R ~ W^0.39 rather than the pure Hopkinson
exponent 1/3. This is NOT a violation of Buckingham — it is an EMERGENT effect
of the canyon channeling mechanism.

Physical explanation: the canyon term contributes C3·sqrt(rho)·(H/s)·W^(1/3)/s
to Z = R/W^(1/3). This term grows like W^(1/3), so R contains a component that
grows as W^(1/3) × W^(1/3) = W^(2/3). Weighted by the actual config parameters
this interpolates between W^(1/3) (no channeling) and W^(2/3) (pure channeling),
giving an effective exponent around 0.39–0.44 for high-Pi geometries.

The formula captures this automatically without a free exponent: R ~ W^(1/3)
is the Hopkinson baseline, and the canyon term adds an urban amplification that
scales as W^(1/3+1/3)/s, boosting the effective exponent for narrow-street
tall-building configs.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. TWO REGIME SUMMARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

REGIME A — SMALL CHARGE  (Π₂ = s/W^(1/3) > 1):
  Primary driver: density rho (plan area density).
  Secondary driver: building SIZE b (wave absorbed by first building row).
  Height H: nearly irrelevant — wave does not reach walls above street level.
  Density effect sign: positive (denser city → larger radius; reflective feeding).
  Practical range in this dataset: W=50 at all street widths; W=250 at s=8 or 12.

REGIME B — LARGE CHARGE  (Π₂ = s/W^(1/3) < 1):
  Primary driver: canyon aspect ratio H/s (Π₃).
  Secondary: wall continuity sqrt(rho) (only as channeling efficiency multiplier).
  Density effect: fades and can reverse sign (frontal blocking).
  Height effect sign: positive for channeling (narrow s), negative for blocking
  (wide s, even in regime B since s may still be > W^(1/3)/2 etc.).
  Practical range: W=500 and 1500 at s=5; W=1000 and 1500 at s=8.

REGIME BOUNDARY: Π₂ = 1, i.e., s = W^(1/3).
  At this point, all geometric effects exactly cancel: Z = baseline A (the
  free-field decay distance). The formula gives Z = C0 + 0 + 0 + 0 = C0, and
  the impulse formula gives Z = A · 1^0 = A. Both are the same physical quantity.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. CANONICAL FORM  (applies to both targets)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    R = W^(1/3) · Z

The dimensionless Z differs by target:

══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
  PRESSURE RADIUS  —  Buckingham Pi additive switch model
══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

    Z_P = C0  +  C1 · (s/W^(1/3))
               +  C2 · rho · (s/W^(1/3) − a)
               +  C3 · sqrt(rho) · (H/s) · (W^(1/3)/s − 1)

    a = 1  for street detonation (det=1)
    a = 2  for intersection detonation (det=2)

TERM-BY-TERM PHYSICAL READING:

  C0  — baseline scaled radius (free-field decay distance).
    The convergence radius in the absence of all geometric effects.
    C0(street)=7.8, C0(intersection)=10.1 — larger for det=2 because 4-canyon
    feeding sustains the urban perturbation even at "neutral" geometry.
    C0 ≈ A (see impulse formula): both represent the scaled distance where the
    free-field wave weakens below the level that can sustain a geometry effect.

  C1·(s/W^(1/3))  — venting term   [C1 < 0].
    Wider street → more venting → wave relaxes to free-field sooner → Z drops.
    Narrower street → more confinement → Z rises. This is Π₂ acting directly.
    C1 is ≈ twice as large at intersections (−1.16 vs −0.58): two canyon axes
    vent simultaneously, doubling the effective venting sensitivity.

  C2·rho·(s/W^(1/3) − a)  — density switch term   [C2 > 0].
    Bracket flips sign at s = a·W^(1/3):
    • s > a·W^(1/3): density → more reflections → Z increases (reflective regime).
    • s < a·W^(1/3): density → more frontal blocking → Z decreases (blocking).
    The threshold a=1 (street) and a=2 (intersection) are NOT fitted — they
    follow from geometry (1 vs 2 canyon directions at the detonation point).
    Cross-validation independently confirmed these values beat all alternatives.

  C3·sqrt(rho)·(H/s)·(W^(1/3)/s − 1)  — canyon law term   [C3 > 0].
    Sign switch at s = W^(1/3), independent of a:
    • s < W^(1/3) (large charge): bracket positive → tall canyon channels wave →
      H INCREASES Z.
    • s > W^(1/3) (small charge): bracket negative → tall buildings block/scatter
      → H DECREASES Z slightly.
    H enters only through H/s (canyon shape, not absolute height).
    sqrt(rho) = b/(b+s) is 1-D wall continuity (waveguide leakage through gaps).
    C3 ≈ 0.58–0.71 for both street and intersection: same physical mechanism,
    layout-independent — strong evidence this term captures a real wave effect.

══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
  IMPULSE RADIUS  —  log-space confinement model
══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

    Z_I = A · Pi^( k · ln(W^(1/3)/s) )     where Pi = H/(s·rho)

  Equivalent log-linear form (as a function of Pi groups):
    ln(Z_I) = ln(A)  +  k · ln(Pi) · ln(W^(1/3)/s)
    ln(Π₀)  = ln(A)  −  k · ln(Π_conf) · ln(Π₂)

  This is a BILINEAR model in log-space: the effect of confinement (ln Pi)
  is modulated by the regime (ln W^(1/3)/s), and vice versa.

TERM READING:

  A  — baseline scaled impulse radius (same role as C0 for pressure).
    Numerically ≈ 12 m/kg^(1/3): the Hopkinson-scaled distance at which the
    free-field impulse decays to ~9 kPa·ms — below this level the blast is too
    weak for the urban geometry to sustain a measurable perturbation.
    A is therefore PREDICTABLE from the free-field impulse curve, not a genuinely
    free parameter. This is why A is nearly the same for street and intersection
    (11.9 vs 12.8): both share the same free-field decay threshold.

  k · ln(W^(1/3)/s) = −k · ln(Π₂)  — confinement exponent.
    This is the exponent of Pi. Its sign is determined by the regime:
    • Large charge (W^(1/3) > s): exponent positive → high Pi extends R
      (confinement amplifies urban influence in channeling regime).
    • Small charge (W^(1/3) < s): exponent negative → high Pi shortens R
      (confinement absorbs the small wave in absorption regime).
    • At s = W^(1/3): exponent = 0 → Z = A, geometry-independent.
    The exponent magnitude grows logarithmically as the charge departs from the
    crossover — deeper in each regime, geometry matters more.

  Why log formula beats switch formula for IMPULSE:
    Impulse integrates the full time-history of the positive phase, including
    multiple reflections. This produces a smoother, more gradual geometry
    dependence that is captured better by the continuous log model than by the
    piecewise-linear switch model. The switch model handles pressure well because
    peak overpressure is dominated by the primary shock, which has sharper
    geometric transitions. Both choices were confirmed by head-to-head CV
    comparison (500 identical train/test splits): log wins for I (80% of splits,
    median MAPE 8.25% vs 9.20%), switch wins for P.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10b. Z_URBAN AND R_URBAN — LOAD-LEVEL MAPPING (max_radius_per_Z.csv)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A second model family answers a different question: for a given free-field
load level (the load at scaled distance Z_free), what is the maximum radius
MaxR in the URBAN environment where that load level still occurs?
Z_urban = MaxR/W^(1/3) is the urban-equivalent scaled distance.

CANONICAL FORM (same as the convergence radius):  MaxR = W^(1/3) · Z_urban

    Z_urban = C · Z_free^m · rho^p · (H/s)^q · (s/W^(1/3))^r

    R_urban = W^(1/3) · Z_urban     (derived identically — NOT separately
                                     fitted; its relative errors equal
                                     Z_urban's row-for-row)

DATA EXTRACTION (MaxR):
  MaxR is the 95th percentile (over 91 one-degree angular bins) of the
  outermost cells where the urban field still delivers AT LEAST the
  free-field level (exceedance definition, parameter-free). This replaced
  an earlier tolerance-band matching (±15%/±5% around the level), which
  inflated MaxR by 3–12% near the convergence radius — where urban/ff ≈ 1
  is within the band width — and left angular bins empty in the steep near
  field. The exceedance data is lower by ~7% (P) / ~9–12% (I) on median.

VALIDITY DOMAIN:
  1. Z_free ≥ 2 (both targets): at Z_free = 1 the point lies within one
     Hopkinson length of the charge — inside the first street, where the
     discrete near-field geometry dominates any smooth formula.
  2. Amplification regime only (Z_urban > Z_free): the formula describes how
     the city EXTENDS the reach of each load level. The exceedance data
     revealed a genuine attenuation regime — small charges at intersections
     (W=50, det2, near field), where 4-way venting makes the urban impulse
     WEAKER than free-field (Z_urban < Z_free). These 12 rows (of ~900) are
     a different physics, excluded from fit and validation; for such cases
     the practical estimate is Z_urban ≈ Z_free (urban ≈ free-field or less).

PHYSICAL CLOSURE WITH THE CONVERGENCE RADIUS:
  By definition the urban field equals free-field beyond R_conv, so MaxR can
  never exceed the convergence radius, and never fall below the free-field
  radius Z_free·W^(1/3). Predictions are therefore clipped to

      Z_free  ≤  Z_urban  ≤  Z_conv

  where Z_conv comes from the convergence-radius formulas (RadiusP additive,
  RadiusI log) — the two formula systems form one self-consistent chain.
  For Z_free ≥ Z_conv the mapping is the identity (urban = free-field).
  The clip activates on ~2–5% of rows (those approaching convergence, where
  the raw power law overshoots) and slightly improves the pressure MAPE; the
  fit itself only ever uses rows with MaxR < R_conv (actual).

BUCKINGHAM STATUS:
  A pure power law in the Pi groups (rho, Π₃=H/s, Π₂=s/W^(1/3)) and the
  dimensionless input level Z_free; log-linear, so the fit is a plain OLS in
  log space — same family as the RadiusI convergence formula. W enters only
  through W^(1/3); 5 dimensionless coefficients per (det × target).
  This replaced an earlier model that used raw H in metres and a free W^n
  exponent (which fitted to n≈0 — dead weight).

  MODEL SELECTION (config-level 5-fold CV over candidate families):
  additive Pi brackets, log brackets, geometry-modulated exponents, strict
  transplants of the convergence switch/log shapes, confinement-group
  variants, slenderness (b/H) corrections, and tail-targeted fitting norms
  (L4, minimax/Chebyshev) were all tested out-of-sample. The strict
  switch/log transplant was clearly worse (12.8–16.6% MAPE) — Z_urban is a
  smooth load-level mapping, not a regime boundary, so sign-switching
  brackets do not apply. Tail-targeted norms were strictly worse
  out-of-sample (they overfit the in-sample tail). Slenderness terms
  (rho exponent modulated by ln(b/H)) improved CV MAPE by ~0.3–0.7% but
  were dropped in favour of the simpler 5-coefficient form.

PHYSICAL READING:
  • m ≈ 1.0 for pressure: the urban field shifts the free-field pressure
    curve nearly proportionally — a roughly constant amplification factor.
  • m ≈ 0.7 for impulse: sub-linear — the urban impulse amplification is
    strongest at small Z_free (near field, reflections still strong) and
    fades toward the convergence radius.
  • C_impulse ≈ 3.5 vs C_pressure ≈ 1.7–2.1: impulse is amplified roughly
    twice as strongly — it integrates all reflections.
  • p > 0 always: density extends the reach of every load level
    (reflective feeding), strongest for street detonations.
  • q ≈ 0 for pressure but q > 0 for impulse: canyon depth barely moves the
    peak-pressure mapping, but deep canyons hold the impulse up farther out
    (channeled reflections keep feeding the time integral).
  • r < 0 for pressure (wide scaled streets vent the peak), r > 0 weakly for
    impulse.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
11. BOTH FORMULAS ARE PHYSICALLY GROUNDED AND BUCKINGHAM-CONSISTENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

What "derived from Buckingham" means in this context:
  The theorem constrains ARGUMENTS (only Pi groups may appear) but not the shape
  of f. A formula is Buckingham-consistent if:
  (1) it depends only on Pi groups identified from dimensional analysis,
  (2) the W^(1/3) Hopkinson scaling is imposed rather than fitted,
  (3) the shape of f is motivated by physical reasoning and confirmed by data.
  Both formulas satisfy all three conditions.

PRESSURE FORMULA — Buckingham audit:
  • C0: dimensionless constant. ✓
  • C1·(s/W^(1/3)): pure Π₂. ✓
  • C2·rho·(s/W^(1/3) − a): rho = f(Π₁,Π₄), multiplied by Π₂, minus a fixed
    geometric number. The bracket is Π₂ − a; "a" is a geometric constant fixed
    by the number of canyon axes (1 or 2). ✓
  • C3·sqrt(rho)·(H/s)·(W^(1/3)/s − 1): sqrt(rho) = f(Π₄), H/s = Π₃,
    (W^(1/3)/s − 1) = (1/Π₂ − 1). All dimensionless. ✓
  • W enters ONLY through W^(1/3). Super-Hopkinson scaling (exponent ~0.39)
    emerges from the canyon term, not a fitted exponent. ✓

IMPULSE FORMULA — Buckingham audit:
  • A: dimensionless. ✓
  • Pi_conf = H/(s·rho) = Π₃·(1 + 1/Π₄)²: valid Pi group. ✓
  • The exponent k·ln(W^(1/3)/s) = −k·ln(Π₂): logarithm of a dimensionless
    argument — dimensionless. ✓
  • W enters only through W^(1/3). ✓
  • Sign flip at Π₂=1 is a dimensionless condition. ✓

ONE HONEST CAVEAT:
  The threshold s = W^(1/3) implicitly equates metres with kg^(1/3). In fully
  rigorous form the boundary is s = (E/p₀)^(1/3). Since all simulations share
  one atmospheric pressure and one explosive type, this prefactor is constant
  and absorbed into the coefficients. For the thesis: the regime boundary is
  s ≈ (E/p₀)^(1/3); the numerical constants express this in TNT-at-sea-level
  units. Generalization to other conditions requires the full (E/p₀)^(1/3) form.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
12. WHAT WAS TESTED AND REJECTED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

• Two separate height terms H/s and H·W^(1/3)/s² → replaced by the single
  factored term (H/s)·(W^(1/3)/s − 1); one parameter saved, same physics.

• Plain rho term alongside the switch term → algebraically collapses to
  rho·(s/W^(1/3) − a); fitting a directly gives a≈1 (street), a≈2 (intersection),
  so a was frozen at those geometric values.

• rho·H/W^(1/3) interaction → rejected (worst-group MAPE 11.3% vs 10.0%;
  high collinearity).

• Hard gate max(0, H/W^(1/3) − 3) on the density term → rejected (unstable;
  only ~20 configs activate it; worst-group MAPE 11.4–11.7% vs 10.1%).

• Free W exponent → unnecessary; fixed 1/3 plus canyon term reproduces the
  observed effective exponent ~0.39 without an extra parameter.

• Log-space power-law Z = C·rho^a·(H/s)^b·(s/W^(1/3))^c for RadiusP → cannot
  represent sign-switching effects (monotonic in each argument). The additive
  form is required by the data.

• Channeling/blocking stratification in CV (4 strata) → replaced by det-only
  stratification (2 strata). Regime is encoded inside the formula terms.

• Switch formula for RadiusI vs log formula: log wins in 80% of 500 CV splits,
  median MAPE 8.25% (log) vs 9.20% (switch). Decision: log for impulse.

• Log formula for RadiusP vs switch formula: switch wins. Decision: switch for P.

• a=√2 and a=1 for intersection → both beaten by a=2 (9.2% vs 10.0% / 10.8%
  worst-group MAPE). Geometric argument (2 canyon axes) confirmed by data.

  (Full 6-coefficient model: 10.8% average, 31.0% worst — extra parameters
   overfit noise; the hybrid formula generalises better.)

WEIGHT EXTRAPOLATION:
  Train on W≤1000, predict W=1500 :  ~5.1%   (excellent)
  Train on W≥250,  predict W=50   :  ~19.1%  (weak — W=50 is regime A
                                              where the formula is imprecise)

DATA/PARAMETER RATIO: 24:1 per location group.
In-sample/out-of-sample gap: ~2%. No evidence of overfitting.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
14. LIMITATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

• W=50 kg: ~19% average error. At small charges the radius is governed by the
  discrete geometry of the first building row (wave absorbed behind one row);
  the smooth formula cannot capture this. Formula is reliable for W ≥ 250 kg.

• Largest residuals: W=50 at intersection (up to 3.2 Z-units, e.g.,
  det2_b30_s20_h4_w50) and b=10 groups at W=250.

• All data come from idealized periodic geometry simulations (uniform grids of
  identical rectangular buildings). Validity for realistic irregular urban
  layouts has not been tested.

• The constants C0, a, and A implicitly assume TNT at standard atmospheric
  pressure. Generalization: replace W^(1/3) with (E/p₀)^(1/3) throughout.

• The formula captures CONVERGENCE RADIUS — not the peak pressure or impulse
  at a given point, only the distance at which the urban and free-field fields
  become indistinguishable. Inside the convergence radius the actual urban
  amplification (channeling peaks, shadow regions) is not modelled.
