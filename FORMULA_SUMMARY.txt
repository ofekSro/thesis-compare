================================================================================
  CONVERGENCE RADIUS FORMULA — FULL SUMMARY
  From Buckingham Pi theorem to the final fitted model (compare_v6)
================================================================================

The convergence radius R is the distance from the detonation point beyond which
the blast field in the urban environment is indistinguishable from the
free-field blast (urban/free-field ratio converges to 1). Two radii are
computed per configuration: RadiusP (peak overpressure) and RadiusI (impulse).


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. BUCKINGHAM PI DERIVATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Physical variables governing the problem:

    R   [m]    convergence radius (dependent variable)
    E   [J]    charge energy  — proportional to charge mass W [kg TNT]
    p0  [Pa]   ambient pressure
    b   [m]    building size
    s   [m]    street width
    H   [m]    building height

6 variables, 3 independent dimensions (M, L, T)
   →  6 − 3 = 3 independent dimensionless groups + 1 dependent group.

Choosing E and p0 as the repeating variables gives the natural length scale

    L* = (E/p0)^(1/3)  ∝  W^(1/3)        (Hopkinson–Cranz scaling law)

so every length in the problem is measured against W^(1/3). The four
dimensionless groups are:

    Pi_0 = R / W^(1/3)   = Z      (scaled convergence radius — the target)
    Pi_1 = rho = b^2/(b+s)^2      (plan area density of the urban fabric)
    Pi_2 = s / W^(1/3)            (scaled street width)
    Pi_3 = H / s                  (street-canyon aspect ratio)

Buckingham's theorem then guarantees a relation of the form

    Z = f(rho, s/W^(1/3), H/s)

with f a dimensionless function to be determined from the data. The theorem
fixes WHAT the formula can depend on; the data tells us the SHAPE of f.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE FINAL FORMULA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Z = R / W^(1/3) = C0
                    + C1 * (s / W^(1/3))
                    + C2 * rho * (s/W^(1/3) - a)
                    + C3 * sqrt(rho) * (H/s) * (W^(1/3)/s - 1)

    R = W^(1/3) * Z

    a = 1  for street detonation (det=1)
    a = 2  for intersection detonation (det=2)

Four fitted constants (C0..C3) per detonation type per target; "a" is a fixed
geometric constant, not fitted.

Fitted coefficients (best CV iteration, fixed seed):

  Pressure radius:
    Street       : Z = +7.591 - 0.507*(s/W^1/3) + 2.120*rho*(s/W^1/3 - 1) + 0.620*sqrt(rho)*(H/s)*(W^1/3/s - 1)
    Intersection : Z = +10.135 - 1.184*(s/W^1/3) + 2.213*rho*(s/W^1/3 - 2) + 0.628*sqrt(rho)*(H/s)*(W^1/3/s - 1)

  Impulse radius:
    Street       : Z = +12.383 - 0.570*(s/W^1/3) + 1.141*rho*(s/W^1/3 - 1) + 0.536*sqrt(rho)*(H/s)*(W^1/3/s - 1)
    Intersection : Z = +13.475 - 0.516*(s/W^1/3) + 0.401*rho*(s/W^1/3 - 2) + 0.379*sqrt(rho)*(H/s)*(W^1/3/s - 1)


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. PHYSICAL MEANING OF EACH TERM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

* C0  — baseline scaled radius.
  - The convergence radius of a "generic" urban area in Hopkinson units.
  - Intersection baseline (10.1 / 13.5) is larger than street baseline
    (7.6 / 12.4): at an intersection the blast feeds four canyons instead of
    two, so urban influence reaches farther before the field relaxes to
    free-field.
  - Impulse baselines are larger than pressure baselines: impulse, being a
    time integral, "remembers" reflections and channeled flow much longer than
    the peak overpressure does, so it converges to free-field farther out.

* C1 * (s/W^(1/3))  — scaled street width (venting term).   C1 < 0 everywhere.
  - A wider street relative to the charge size lets the blast expand and vent
    almost freely; the city perturbs the wave less, so the urban field matches
    free-field sooner (smaller Z).
  - Conversely, a street that is narrow compared with W^(1/3) confines the
    wave, prolonging the urban influence.
  - This is the dimensionless statement of "confinement vs venting".

* C2 * rho * (s/W^(1/3) - a)  — density switch term.   C2 > 0 everywhere.
  - rho = b^2/(b+s)^2 is the fraction of ground covered by buildings.
  - The bracket flips the SIGN of the density effect at s = a * W^(1/3):
      - Near-field regime (s < a*W^(1/3), bracket negative):
        buildings stand inside the strong-blast region. A denser city means
        more frontal blocking and energy absorption close to the charge —
        the urban field decays to free-field SOONER (rho reduces Z).
      - Far-field regime (s > a*W^(1/3), bracket positive):
        the street is wide relative to the charge; buildings act as
        reflectors/guides rather than blockers, and a denser city keeps
        feeding reflections — rho INCREASES Z.
  - The threshold "a" is a geometric constant:
      a = 1 (street):       the single canyon vents when s exceeds one
                            Hopkinson length.
      a = 2 (intersection): two orthogonal canyons vent simultaneously,
                            doubling the effective venting threshold.
    CV confirmed a=2 beats a=sqrt(2) and a=1 for det=2 (9.2% vs 10.0% vs
    10.8% worst-group MAPE) — the data independently selects the value that
    the geometry argument predicts.

* C3 * sqrt(rho) * (H/s) * (W^(1/3)/s - 1)  — canyon law term.  C3 > 0 everywhere.
  - This is the building-height physics, and it carries its own sign switch
    at s = W^(1/3):
      - Narrow streets (s < W^(1/3), bracket positive): the canyon acts as a
        waveguide. Taller buildings trap and channel the wave (multiple wall
        reflections, no relief over the roofline), pushing the convergence
        radius OUT. Channeling regime: H increases R.
      - Wide streets (s > W^(1/3), bracket negative): the wave clears the
        buildings; tall buildings now act mainly as isolated obstacles whose
        shadows and rarefactions disturb the near field but bleed energy —
        H REDUCES R. Blocking regime.
  - H enters through the aspect ratio H/s — it is the canyon shape, not the
    absolute height, that matters (a 24 m building over a 36 m street is a
    "low" canyon; over a 5 m street it is a deep slot).
  - sqrt(rho) = b/(b+s) is the 1-D wall continuity along the canyon — the
    fraction of the street wall that is actually built. Channeling is a
    waveguide phenomenon: what matters is how continuous the guiding walls
    are (1-D measure), not how much ground area is covered (2-D measure).
    Cross-street gaps "leak" the guided wave, so the channeling strength
    scales with b/(b+s), i.e. with sqrt(rho).
  - The canyon coefficient C3 for pressure is virtually identical for street
    (0.620) and intersection (0.628) — strong evidence the term captures a
    real, layout-independent mechanism.

* Super-Hopkinson scaling (emergent, not imposed):
  - Free fits of an effective exponent give R ~ W^0.39 rather than W^(1/3),
    growing with H. The formula reproduces this automatically: the canyon
    term contains W^(1/3)/s, so larger charges deepen the channeling
    contribution on top of the basic W^(1/3) scale-up. No extra exponent
    parameter is needed.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. PATTERNS OBSERVED IN THE DATA (and how the formula encodes them)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

(All patterns read from convergence_table.csv, 72 original configs,
 weights 50/500/1500 kg; W=250 and 1000 kept aside for validation plots.)

* Charge weight is the dominant variable.
  R grows monotonically with W for every geometry, approximately as W^(1/3)
  (slightly faster for tall, narrow-street configs — see super-Hopkinson
  above). Encoded by: the overall W^(1/3) prefactor + canyon term.

* Intersections reach farther than streets.
  For matching geometry, det=2 radii exceed det=1 radii. Four feeding canyons
  vs two. Encoded by: larger C0 for det=2.

* Impulse converges farther out than pressure.
  RadiusI > RadiusP in nearly every configuration. Impulse integrates the
  whole positive phase including late reflections. Encoded by: larger C0 and
  smaller correction coefficients for the impulse fits.

* Height effect flips sign with street width.
  - s = 5 m (deep canyons): raising H from 4 to 24 m INCREASES R
    (channeling).
  - s = 20-36 m (open layouts): raising H DECREASES R or leaves it flat
    (blocking).
  - The crossover street width grows with the charge: heavier charges keep
    "narrow-street behaviour" up to wider streets.
  Encoded by: the (W^(1/3)/s - 1) bracket of the canyon term — one term, one
  coefficient, sign switch at s = W^(1/3).

* Density effect flips sign with scaled street width, and fades with charge.
  - Small W (50 kg, large s/W^(1/3)): going b=15 → b=30 (higher rho)
    INCREASES R — reflective feeding.
  - Large W (1500 kg, small s/W^(1/3)): higher rho DECREASES R or does
    nothing — frontal blocking close to the charge.
  - The effect is strongest for pressure at intersections; for impulse it is
    weak (small C2 in the det=2 impulse fit).
  Encoded by: the rho*(s/W^(1/3) - a) switch term.

* Density matters mainly when buildings are tall relative to the charge.
  The rho effect is clearly visible when H/W^(1/3) is large (small charges,
  tall buildings) and washes out for big charges that overtop the buildings.
  A hard gate max(0, H/W^(1/3) - 3) was tested and REJECTED — too few configs
  activate it, the coefficient is unstable in CV. The smooth switch term
  captures most of the same behaviour through s/W^(1/3).

* Why the raw R-vs-term graphs can look "contradictory".
  In a fixed-geometry line only W varies, and W moves BOTH axes (x contains
  W^(1/3); R contains the full Hopkinson scale-up). The apparent negative
  trend of R against the switch term is W confounding, not the term's sign.
  In Z-space (W^(1/3) divided out) the terms act with the fitted signs; the
  Z-graphs are noisier precisely because they show only the residual urban
  physics (~8-9% measurement noise on a much smaller dynamic range).
  Recommended thesis use: R-plots to tell the physical story, Z-plots (or
  partial-residual plots) to justify the formula structure.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. WHAT WAS TESTED AND REJECTED ALONG THE WAY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

* Two separate height terms, H/s and H*W^(1/3)/s^2  →  replaced by the single
  factored canyon law (H/s)*(W^(1/3)/s - 1); one parameter saved, same
  physics, better CV stability.
* Plain rho term alongside the switch term (C*rho + C'*rho*s/W^(1/3))
  →  algebraically collapses to the switch form rho*(s/W^(1/3) - a) with
  a = -C/C'; fitting a directly per layout gave a ≈ 1 (street) and
  a ≈ 2 (intersection), so "a" was frozen at those geometric values.
* rho * H/W^(1/3) interaction  →  rejected (worst-group MAPE 11.3% vs 10.0%;
  high collinearity with existing H and W terms).
* Hard gate max(0, H/W^(1/3) - 3) on the density term  →  rejected (unstable:
  only ~20 configs activate it; CV worst-group MAPE 11.4-11.7% vs 10.1%).
* Free W exponent  →  unnecessary; fixed 1/3 plus the canyon term reproduces
  the observed effective exponent ~0.39.
* Log-space power-law fit Z = C*rho^a*(H/s)^b*(s/W^(1/3))^c  →  cannot
  represent sign-switching effects (a power law is monotonic in each group);
  the additive form with switching brackets is required by the data.
